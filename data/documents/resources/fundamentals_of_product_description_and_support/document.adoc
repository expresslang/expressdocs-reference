   <schema name="basic_attribute_schema"
           number="10466"
           number.supersedes="9686"
           version="9">
      <introduction>
			The subject of the <b>basic_attribute_schema</b> is the assignment of id, name, description and role attributes to
			aspects of product data.
			This schema defines the mechanism to define a value for these attributes and associate it with product data.
			<note number="1">
				The concepts defined in this schema do not provide a generic capability to associate an identification, a
				name, a description, or a role to product data.
				They are applied, in order to ensure upward compatibility, in the case where a requirement to add one or more of these kinds
				of attributes to a particular entity data type has been identified during the revision or amendment process
				of the standard document in which this entity data type is defined.
			</note>
         <note number="2">
				The associated entities are defined in ISO 10303-41, ISO 10303-43 and ISO 10303-44.
			</note>
      </introduction>
      <fund_cons>
         <p>The following concepts are supported by this schema:</p>
         <ul>
            <li>the assignment of an identifier to product data;</li>
            <li>the assignment of a label to product data;</li>
            <li>the assignment of a descriptive text to product data;</li>
            <li>the assignment of a role to an association of management type data with other aspects of product data.</li>
         </ul>
         <p>
				The assignment of an identifier corresponds to the <b>id_attribute</b> entity data type.
				The entities to which an identifier may be assigned are specified in the <b>id_attribute_select</b> data type.
				These entities have a derived attribute <b>id</b>.
				The corresponding identifier value is provided by the <b>get_id_value</b> function.
				A constraint ensures that there is at most one instance of <b>id_attribute</b> assigned to an instance of an entity in the
				<b>id_attribute_select</b> type.
			</p>
         <p>
				The assignment of a name corresponds to the <b>name_attribute</b> entity data type.
				The entities to which a name may be assigned are specified in the <b>name_attribute_select</b> data type.
				These entities have a derived attribute name.
				The corresponding label value is provided by the <b>get_name_value</b> function.
				A constraint ensures that there is at most one instance of <b>name_attribute</b> assigned to an instance of an entity in the
				<b>name_attribute_select</b> type.
			</p>
         <p>
				The assignment of a descriptive text corresponds to the <b>description_attribute</b> entity data type.
				The entities to which a description may be assigned are specified in the <b>description_attribute_select</b> data type.
				These entities have a derived attribute description.
				The corresponding text value is provided by the <b>get_description_value</b> function.
				A constraint ensures that there is at most one instance of <b>description_attribute</b> assigned to an instance of
				an entity in the <b>description_attribute_select</b> type.
			</p>
         <p>
				The assignment of a role corresponds to the <b>role_association</b> entity data type.
				The entities to which a role may be assigned are specified in the <b>role_select</b> data type.
				These entities have a derived attribute role.
				The corresponding <b>object_role</b> is provided by the <b>get_role</b> function.
				A constraint ensures that there is at most one instance of <b>role_association</b> assigned to an instance of an entity
				in the <b>role_select</b> type.
			</p>
      </fund_cons>

   <schema name="product_definition_schema"
           number="10467"
           number.supersedes="9684"
           version="9">
      <introduction>
			The subject of the <b>product_definition_schema</b> is the identification of products, the categorization of
			products, definitions of products, and the relationships among them. This schema provides for the generic
			aspects of product definition.
			<example>
				The identification of products and definitions of products, the grouping of products according to
				classification schemes and the definition of various kinds of relationship between products are all examples of
				generic aspects of product definition.
			</example>
      </introduction>
      <fund_cons>
         <p>
			The use of the entity data types of this schema to represent products is specified in the viewpoint as defined
			by the requirements of an annotated EXPRESS schema that uses or specializes this part of ISO 10303.
			Every product need not be represented by an instance of the entity data type <b>product</b>.
			Some products may be represented by other constructs in the integrated resources.
			</p>
         <example number="1">
				The constructs <b>product_definition_formation</b> and <b>product_definition</b>, defined in the <b>product_definition_schema</b>
				of this part of ISO 10303, are examples of other entity data types that may represent a product.
			</example>
         <example number="2">
				The construct <b>action_resource</b>, defined in ISO 10303-49, is an example of an entity data type that may represent a product.
			</example>
         <p>
			A single product may have multiple groups of definitions associated with it; each group is valid in a given application context.
			</p>
         <example number="3">
				An application context may be the manufacture of ball-point pens.
				A particular design of ball-point pen has multiple versions. Each version is described through a group of product definitions.
				An older version has a solid cap whereas a more recent version has a hole in the cap to prevent people from choking
				if they swallow it.
				The characteristics of the two versions would be different from each other because the later version would have a hole in
				the cap whereas the earlier version would not.
				This schema could be used to define the ball-point pen with a solid cap as one product and the one with a hole in the cap
				as another.
				On the other hand it could also be used to define both pens as two versions of a single product.
				The approach taken would depend upon the requirements of the application context.
			</example>
         <p>
			The definition of a product may include the properties that characterize it.
			</p>
         <example number="4">
				An integrated circuit product will have a functional definition represented by a circuit schematic diagram and
				a physical definition represented by a circuit layout diagram.
			</example>
         <p>
			Definitions of products may be related to each other in various ways.
			This part of ISO 10303 provides the resources needed to identify the relationships among products
			and to be able to characterize those relationships.
			</p>
         <example number="5">
				The ball-point pen cap would be related in an assembly relationship to the ball-point pen itself and
				one of the caps could be substituted for the other in some circumstances.
			</example>
         <p>
			There are two ways to represent the concept of real object:
			</p>
         <ul>
            <li>
					a real object can be represented by an instance of <b>product</b> if the subject of an annotated Express
					schema is concerned with real objects or if it is needed to describe a real object at various
					life-cycle stages and in various configurations;
					in this case, if an real object is identified by a serial number, this serial number can be stored in the id attribute
					of the <b>product</b>;
					<example number="6">
						An airplane, in service during 30 years, may have different configurations during its life.
					</example>
            </li>
            <li>
					a real object can be represented by an instance of <b>product_definition</b> if the subject of an
					annotated Express schema is primarily concerned with the design phase of products and if the
					concept of real object is considered as a particular stage in the design phase of a product;
					in this case, if a real object is identified by a serial number, this <b>serial_number</b> can be stored in the id
					attribute of the <b>product_definition</b>.
					<example number="7">
						A prototype may be considered as a particular definition of a product.
					</example>
            </li>
         </ul>
         <p>
			Product definition facts are independent of properties.
			The ways in which these facts may be combined are prescribed by the relationships that are defined in this schema.
			Each product definition fact can be interpreted in any application context;
			the ways in which these facts are to be interpreted are prescribed in annotated EXPRESS schemas that use or specialise
			the resources defined in this part of ISO 10303.
			</p>
         <note>
				An example of the way in which this schema may be used is given in annex F.1.
			</note>
      </fund_cons>

   <schema name="product_property_definition_schema"
           number="10467"
           number.supersedes="9684"
           version="9">
      <introduction>
			The subject of the <b>product_property_definition_schema</b> is the characteristics of an object.
		</introduction>
      <fund_cons>
         <p>
			This schema provides resources for the identification of:
			</p>
         <ul>
            <li>types of properties to be identified and described;</li>
            <li>properties assigned to a <b>characterized_object</b>, a <b>product_definition</b>, a <b>product_definition_relationship</b>
				or a <b>shape_definition</b>.</li>
         </ul>
         <p>
			The meaning of each assigned property may be defined either by its association with an instance of the entity characterizing
			a type of property or by its name attribute.
			</p>
         <example number="1">
				The definition of the surface finish of a shape is independent of the way in which the shape or the surface
				finish is represented.
			</example>
         <note>
				In the previous example, both the defined surface finish and the shape could have multiple representations.
			</note>
         <p>
			Each characteristic may be associated with a single <b>characterized_object</b> or <b>product_definition</b> or with
			one <b>product_definition</b> in the context of another.
			Each characteristic is independent of the number or types of representations of that characteristic.
			Each characteristic may be associated with the shape of a product, an element of the shape of a product,
			or the relationship between elements of the shape of a product.
			</p>
         <p>
			This part of ISO 10303 distinguishes between the definition of a product and its possible usages.
			This part of ISO 10303 establishes the following assumptions:
			</p>
         <ul>
            <li>
               <p>the definition of a given object is characterized by a set of unique properties.</p>
               <example number="2">
						A product cannot have two shapes simultaneously.
					</example>
            </li>
            <li>
               <p>any usage of the object is characterized by a set of unique properties.</p>
               <example number="3">
						A product, like glue, may have different shapes depending on its usage.
					</example>
            </li>
            <li>
               <p>a property characterizes either the definition or one of the usages of an object.</p>
               <example number="4">
						The appearance of chair x is a unique property of that chair.
